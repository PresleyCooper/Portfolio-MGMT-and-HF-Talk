Speaker notes

About 40 minutes plus Q&A. Open this on your phone or a second screen. Back to the talk

Title 1 min

  • Point out the color key. It's consistent all night: blue is market/beta, green is long, orange is short, gold is alpha.

01 · Correlation 4 min

  • Before each step, ask the room to predict: "what happens to the curve when I drop correlation to zero?"
  • The gold line is the risk you removed: the gap between the weighted average and the actual mix.
  • Land the line: correlation is the only free variable, and it's what the rest of the night is about.

02 · The free lunch 5 min

  • Idiosyncratic = stock-specific = diversifiable. Systematic = market = non-diversifiable. Same thing, three names each.
  • Key line: since anyone can diversify idiosyncratic risk for free, the market won't pay a premium for it. You're only paid for systematic risk (CAPM, no formula needed).
  • "Diversification is the only free lunch" is usually attributed to Markowitz.

03 · Efficient frontier 6 min

  • Sector numbers are illustrative, not real data. Say so if asked.
  • Step through: sectors, then the cloud, then the frontier, then the capital market line.
  • Separation idea: in theory everyone holds the same risky portfolio and just picks how much cash or leverage.
  • Drag the cash rate and the tangency point moves. Bridge: institutions deviate from the market portfolio on purpose, and that's active management.

04 · Alpha vs. beta 5 min

  • Return = beta × market + alpha + noise. This is the hinge of the whole talk.
  • Leveraged beta: the Sharpe ratio looks fine, but Jensen's alpha is zero. Treynor uses beta instead of total risk.
  • Market neutral: the information ratio is how allocators score it. Above roughly 0.5 is considered good.
  • Close with: a hedge fund should be selling you alpha, not beta. Chapter 10 checks whether they do.

05 · Allocation vs. selection 4 min

  • Brinson-Fachler: allocation = (wp − wb)(rb − Rb). Underweighting a sector that lagged also counts as positive allocation (Energy here).
  • BHB 1986 nuance: allocation policy explains most of the variance of returns over time, not "90% of returns". Knowing this distinction makes you sound sharp.
  • Institutions decide allocation themselves and hire managers for selection. A hedged equity fund is a pure selection bet.
  • If time allows, have someone call out weights and type them into the table live.

06 · The 60/40 problem 3 min

  • 94% of the risk comes from 60% of the dollars (assumes 16% stock vol, 6% bond vol, zero correlation).
  • 2008: S&P −37.0%, Agg +5.2%. 2022: S&P −18.1%, Agg −13.0%. These are real calendar-year total returns. Hover any bar for the year.
  • Why 2022 happened: inflation and rate hikes hit stocks and bonds at the same time.

07 · Shorting flips the sign 4 min

  • Short return = −(stock return), so beta and correlation flip sign.
  • The catch: negative expected return, borrow fees, unlimited losses, squeezes (GameStop, January 2021).
  • Pair slider: at $130 short, beta-adjusted net is zero and correlation to the market is about zero. What remains is the alpha spread.

08 · Shorts and the Sharpe ratio 2 min

Ask before showing step 2: "Would you add a position you expect to lose money on?"
  • Walk the three bars: A alone, A plus the short in B, then scaled up to the same 30% risk.
  • Excess return falls from 7.0% to 4.2%, volatility falls from 30% to 15.2%, Sharpe rises from 0.23 to 0.28. Levered back to 30% vol it earns 8.4%.
  • Why it works: the short removes risk that wasn't being compensated. This is the logic behind every long/short book, which is the next section.

09 · Equity strategies 7 min

  • One dial: beta-adjusted net exposure. Gross = how much risk is on. Net = how much market direction is on.
  • Long-only shows 110% beta-adjusted because the longs have a beta of 1.1.
  • Click the market scenario buttons on each preset. Market neutral barely moves in a −30% crash.
  • Mention sub-styles: fundamental (concentrated, analyst-driven), quant/stat arb (hundreds of names, factor-neutral), pairs.
  • Pairs: dollar-neutral loses money in a +20% sector rally even though Booking beat Expedia. Beta-neutral is flat. Numbers are illustrative.

10 · The allocator's view 3 min

  • 2 and 20 on 6% of gross alpha leaves you about 43% of it. At 1.5 and 17, about 54%.
  • Fee levels: check a current industry survey before quoting average terms.
  • Sleeve: 20% in a market-neutral fund lifts Sharpe and cuts the equity share of risk. Flip to the 2022 regime to show it matters more when stock-bond correlation turns positive.
  • Also worth naming: portable alpha (cheap beta via futures plus a market-neutral alpha stream), and lockups and gates.
  • Before presenting, consider looking up current correlations of equity hedge and equity market neutral indices to the S&P 500 (HFR publishes these). Long-biased equity hedge has historically been much more correlated than market neutral.

11 · Summary 1 min + Q&A

  • Read the summary sentence out loud.
  • Open the discussion question instead of asking "any questions?"

Presenting tips

  • Arrow keys, Space or Page Down step through. Most clickers work out of the box.
  • P toggles projector mode (bigger type, hides the "show the math" boxes). D toggles dark mode.
  • Every step sets its chart automatically, so you never have to fumble a slider. You can still drag anything live.
  • If a slider has focus, the arrow keys move the slider. Click on the page background to get back to stepping.
  • Backup: download the repo as a ZIP and open index.html. Everything works offline except the web fonts and QR code.
